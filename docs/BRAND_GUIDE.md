## 1. Brand Identity and Foundation

### 1.1 Brand Name

**Primary Recommendation: GharKa**

"GharKa" (Hindi: "from home") is the strongest choice. It immediately communicates the core proposition -- food made at home, by real people, for their neighbors. The name works because:

- It is short, memorable, and easy to say aloud in conversation between neighbors.
- It carries inherent warmth. "Ghar" (home) is one of the most emotionally loaded words in Indian culture.
- It sets the right expectation: this is home food, not restaurant food. That distinction is critical for managing trust and quality expectations from day one.
- It is culturally rooted without being exclusionary. Non-Hindi speakers still grasp the warmth from context.

**Alternative Names Considered (and why GharKa wins)**

| Name | Meaning | Strength | Weakness |
|------|---------|----------|----------|
| **GharKa** | "From home" | Warm, clear, culturally resonant | Hindi-specific, though widely understood |
| **Padosi Plate** | "Neighbor's Plate" | Playful, community-focused | Slightly long, harder to type quickly |
| **Dastarkhwan** | "Spread/feast cloth" (Urdu) | Beautiful word, evokes sharing | Hard to spell, narrow cultural reach |

**Verdict**: GharKa is the name. It is short enough for an app icon, warm enough for a community product, and honest enough for a platform that connects rather than guarantees.

### 1.2 Tagline

**Primary Tagline**:
> "Your neighbor's kitchen, one tap away."

**Supporting Taglines** (for use in different contexts):

| Context | Tagline |
|---------|---------|
| App Store listing | "Homemade food from neighbors you trust." |
| Onboarding screen | "Good food lives next door." |
| Seller-facing | "Share what you cook. Feed your community." |
| Social media / casual | "Because the best food is already in your colony." |

The primary tagline does three things simultaneously: it establishes proximity ("neighbor"), it signals convenience ("one tap"), and it frames the product honestly -- you are accessing someone's kitchen, not ordering from a restaurant.

### 1.3 Brand Purpose

**Why GharKa exists beyond the product:**

Every gated community has home cooks whose food is legendary within their building -- the aunty whose biryani disappears at potlucks, the uncle whose pickle is better than anything on a store shelf, the student who bakes on weekends. That talent and love for cooking has no platform. Meanwhile, neighbors three floors up eat delivery food they do not enjoy because they do not know what is being made twenty meters away.

GharKa exists to close that gap. Not as a food delivery service. Not as a marketplace with ratings and refunds. As a simple, honest bridge between people who love to cook and people who love to eat, living in the same community.

### 1.4 Brand Vision

To make home-cooked food the most accessible, trusted, and celebrated food option within every residential community in India.

### 1.5 Brand Mission

GharKa connects neighbors who cook with neighbors who are hungry, inside gated communities, through a simple app that makes discovering, ordering, and sharing homemade food feel as natural as knocking on a neighbor's door.

### 1.6 Brand Values

These values govern every design decision, every line of copy, and every feature prioritization.

| Value | Definition | How It Shows Up |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Honesty First** | We never overstate what we are. We are a connector, not a guarantor. | Disclaimers are clear. No fake "assured quality" badges. No pretending to be Swiggy. |
| **Neighborly Warmth** | Every interaction should feel like talking to someone you share a lift with. | Friendly copy, first names, no corporate stiffness. |
| **Simplicity** | If a feature needs a tutorial, it is too complex. | Minimal UI, obvious flows, no feature bloat. |
| **Community Ownership** | The community is the product. We are just the tool. | Community-driven moderation, seller autonomy, no heavy-handed platform rules. |
| **Respect for the Cook** | Home cooks are not "vendors." They are neighbors sharing a skill. | Language never reduces cooks to commercial entities. No "restaurant" framing. |

### 1.7 Brand Personality

If GharKa were a person, they would be the friendly neighbor who moved into your building six months ago and already knows everyone by name. They organize the Diwali potluck. They are warm but not overbearing, helpful but not pushy, honest but never harsh.

| Trait | Description | Not This |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| **Warm** | Feels like a conversation, not a transaction | Not saccharine or fake-cheerful |
| **Trustworthy** | Honest about what it is and is not | Not corporate-trust-badge performative |
| **Homely** | Feels like home, not a storefront | Not rustic or kitschy |
| **Community-first** | Celebrates the collective, not individual sellers | Not marketplace-competitive |
| **Simple** | Clear, unburdened, breathing room | Not minimal-for-aesthetic-sake |

### 1.8 Brand Positioning Statement

For residents of gated communities who want homemade food options beyond their own kitchen, GharKa is a neighbor-to-neighbor food sharing app that connects home cooks with hungry neighbors in the same community. Unlike food delivery platforms, GharKa does not operate as a marketplace with guarantees -- it is a community tool that makes discovering and requesting homemade food as simple and personal as asking your neighbor directly.

### 1.10 Typography

All fonts are from Google Fonts for free, reliable, cross-platform usage.

**Primary Typeface -- Headings**

**Nunito** (Google Fonts)
- Weight range: 600 (SemiBold) for subheadings, 700 (Bold) for main headings, 800 (ExtraBold) for hero/display text.
- Why: Rounded terminals give it warmth without sacrificing readability. It feels friendly and approachable -- like handwriting that grew up and got organized. It avoids the cold geometry of Montserrat and the overuse of Poppins.

**Secondary Typeface -- Body Text**

**Inter** (Google Fonts)
- Weight range: 400 (Regular) for body, 500 (Medium) for emphasis, 600 (SemiBold) for labels.
- Why: Designed specifically for screens. Excellent legibility at small sizes (critical for food descriptions and disclaimers). Tall x-height makes it readable on mobile. Neutral enough to let Nunito headings carry the personality.

**Accent Typeface -- Special Use Only**

**Caveat** (Google Fonts)
- Weight: 400 (Regular) only.
- Usage: VERY SPARINGLY. Only for handwritten-feel elements like "Made with love by [Name]" labels, empty state illustrations, or the occasional playful callout. Never for body text, never for navigation, never for more than one element per screen.
- Why: Adds a personal, handmade touch that reinforces the "homemade" ethos. But overuse would make the app feel unpolished.

**Type Scale**

| Level | Font | Size | Weight | Line Height | Usage |
|-------|------|------|--------|-------------|-------|
| Display | Nunito | 28px | 800 | 1.2 | Hero text, onboarding headlines |
| H1 | Nunito | 24px | 700 | 1.3 | Screen titles |
| H2 | Nunito | 20px | 700 | 1.3 | Section headers |
| H3 | Nunito | 17px | 600 | 1.4 | Card titles, seller names |
| Body Large | Inter | 16px | 400 | 1.5 | Primary body text |
| Body | Inter | 14px | 400 | 1.5 | Standard body text, descriptions |

**Font Loading Strategy**

Total weight: ~180KB. Load Nunito and Inter immediately. Lazy-load Caveat since it is used sparingly.

## 2. Trust and Disclaimer Framework

This is the most important section of the brand guide. GharKa has no payment gateway, no food quality guarantees, and no delivery infrastructure. The app is a facilitator -- a digital notice board for home cooks in a gated community. This must be communicated clearly enough to protect the platform legally and set correct user expectations, while being warm enough to not scare people away.

### 2.1 The Core Communication Principle

**"Honest neighbor, not fine print."**

Every disclaimer in GharKa should sound like a neighbor explaining how things work over chai, not like a lawyer covering liability. The information is the same. The feeling is completely different.

Compare:
- **Bad (corporate/legal)**: "GharKa assumes no liability for the quality, safety, or timeliness of food listed on this platform. All transactions are conducted solely between the buyer and seller."
- **Good (GharKa voice)**: "We connect you with neighbors who cook. The food, the price, and the pickup -- that's between you and them. We're just the app that helped you find each other."

Both say the same thing. One makes you want to close the app. The other makes you nod and move on.

### 2.2 The Disclaimer Hierarchy

Not all disclaimers need equal weight. Some need to be acknowledged once. Others should be ambient reminders.

| Level | Where | Purpose | Treatment |
|-------|-------|---------|-----------|
| **Level 1: Full Acknowledgment** | Onboarding (one-time) | Legal protection, expectation setting | Full screen, must scroll and tap "I understand" |
| **Level 2: Contextual Reminder** | Order confirmation | Reinforce at the moment of commitment | Inline text within the flow, not a blocker |
| **Level 3: Ambient Signal** | Listing cards, seller profiles | Subtle ongoing reminder | Small footer text, iconography, tone |
| **Level 4: Reference** | About page, settings | Full legal text available on demand | Detailed, formal, always accessible |

### 2.3 Exact Disclaimer Copy

#### Onboarding Screen Disclaimer (Level 1)

This appears once during first-time setup, after the user selects their community and before they see any listings.

**Screen Title**: "Before you dig in -- a quick note."

**Body Copy**:

> GharKa connects you with home cooks in your community. Here's what that means:
>
> **The food is homemade.** It's cooked in your neighbors' kitchens, not commercial setups. Just like food at a potluck or a neighbor's dinner invite.
>
> **We don't handle payments.** You and the cook settle up directly -- cash, UPI, however you both prefer. GharKa doesn't process or hold any money.
>
> **We don't guarantee the food.** We can't verify taste, ingredients, hygiene, or allergens. If you have allergies or dietary restrictions, please talk to the cook directly before ordering.
>
> **Pickup is between you two.** The cook sets the pickup time and location (usually their door). We don't deliver.
>
> **What we do**: We give you a way to discover what your neighbors are cooking, connect with them, and build a food-sharing community in your colony.

**Button**: "Got it, show me what's cooking"

**Design Notes**:
- Use a warm illustration at the top (neighbors sharing food over a fence/balcony).
- Body text in Body Large (16px Inter 400).
- Bold phrases in Inter 600.
- The button uses the Turmeric primary color.
- This screen cannot be skipped. The user must scroll to the bottom before the button becomes active.
- Store acknowledgment timestamp for legal records.

#### Listing View Footer Text (Level 3)

This appears as a subtle line at the bottom of every food listing card.

**Copy**:

> "Homemade by your neighbor. Chat with them for details on ingredients or allergens."

**Design Notes**:
- Body Small (12px Inter 400), Neutral 500 (Ash) color.
- Sits below the listing description, above the "Message Cook" button.
- Includes a small info icon (outlined, 12px) before the text.
- Not intrusive. Reads as helpful context, not a warning.

**Alternative copy for the listing detail page** (when a user taps into a full listing):

> "This is homemade food prepared in [Cook's first name]'s kitchen. GharKa connects you with home cooks -- we don't verify ingredients, allergens, or preparation methods. When in doubt, just ask [Cook's first name] directly."

#### Order Confirmation Text (Level 2)

This appears on the confirmation screen after a user sends an order request to a cook.

**Screen Title**: "You're all set!"

**Body Copy**:

> Your request has been sent to [Cook's first name]. They'll confirm if it's available and let you know pickup details.
>
> **A few things to remember:**
> - Payment is between you and [Cook's first name] -- settle up when you pick up (cash, UPI, or however you both agree).
> - If you have any allergies or dietary needs, message [Cook's first name] before pickup.
> - GharKa helped you connect, but the food and the transaction are between neighbors.
>
> Enjoy your meal!

**Design Notes**:
- Friendly checkmark illustration at top (not a corporate "order confirmed" banner).
- The reminder section is in a light card (Cloud background, subtle border) so it reads as a helpful note, not a warning box.
- "Message [Cook's first name]" is a tappable link that opens the chat.

#### About Page Legal Text (Level 4)

This lives in Settings > About GharKa and serves as the formal legal reference.

**Section Title**: "How GharKa Works"

**Copy**:

> **GharKa is a community food-sharing facilitator.**
>
> We provide a platform for residents of gated communities to discover and connect with neighbors who prepare homemade food. Here is what that means in plain terms:
>
> **GharKa is not a food delivery service.** We do not prepare, handle, deliver, or store any food. All food listed on GharKa is prepared by individual home cooks in their personal kitchens.
>
> **GharKa does not process payments.** All financial transactions occur directly between the buyer and the cook, outside of the GharKa app. We do not collect, hold, or transfer any money on behalf of either party.
>
> **GharKa does not guarantee food quality or safety.** We do not inspect kitchens, verify ingredients, test for allergens, or certify any food listed on the platform. Home-cooked food is not subject to the same regulations as commercial food establishments. Users with food allergies, dietary restrictions, or health concerns should communicate directly with the cook before consuming any food.
>
> **GharKa does not guarantee transactions.** We are not responsible for order fulfillment, cancellations, refunds, or disputes between buyers and cooks. Any issues should be resolved directly between the parties involved.
>
> **GharKa does not verify user identities beyond community membership.** We confirm that users belong to a registered gated community but do not perform background checks, health screenings, or food safety certifications.
>
> **By using GharKa, you acknowledge that:**
> - All food consumption is at your own risk and discretion.
> - All financial transactions are conducted independently between you and the other party.
> - GharKa's role is limited to facilitating discovery and connection between community members.
> - You will communicate directly with cooks regarding any dietary concerns, allergies, or special requirements.
>
> **Community self-governance.** GharKa relies on community trust and self-regulation. If you have concerns about a listing or a user, you can report them through the app, and we will review the report in accordance with our community guidelines.
>
> For questions about this policy, contact us at [support email].

**Design Notes**:
- This is the only place where formal legal language is appropriate.
- Even here, the tone is explanatory rather than adversarial. Sentences explain what things mean, not just what the company is not liable for.
- Use H2 for section title, H3 for bold sub-headers, Body (14px) for text.
- Include a "Last updated: [date]" line at the bottom.

## 3. Seller Information Strategy

### 3.1 The Decision: Detailed Profiles vs. Minimal Profiles

**Recommendation: Progressive disclosure with a "warm minimal" default.**

Do NOT show detailed kitchen photos, certifications, or commercial-style profiles. Here is why:

**Against detailed profiles:**
1. **It creates a false sense of commercial operation.** Kitchen photos and certifications imply inspection and standards. GharKa has neither. Showing these creates an expectation we cannot back up.
2. **It raises the liability bar.** If we display "kitchen photos" as a trust signal, we implicitly endorse cleanliness. When we explicitly say we do not verify kitchens, displaying kitchen photos is contradictory.
3. **It discourages casual cooks.** The aunty who makes amazing pickle on weekends is not going to photograph her kitchen and upload certificates. A detailed profile requirement filters out exactly the cooks that make GharKa valuable.
4. **It turns neighbors into vendors.** The moment you ask for "food safety certifications," you have reframed a neighbor sharing food as a commercial food operation. That changes the entire social dynamic.

**For warm minimal profiles:**
1. **Neighbors already have trust context.** In a gated community, you know (or know of) the people around you. A name, building number, and a friendly photo is often enough.
2. **Ratings and repeat orders build trust organically.** The best trust signal is "12 of your neighbors have had Meena aunty's biryani and all came back for more."
3. **Chat is the trust mechanism.** If someone wants to know about ingredients, kitchen practices, or allergens, they message the cook directly. That conversation IS the trust-building.

### 3.2 What Buyers See (Seller Profile - Public)

| Field | Example | Required? | Notes |
|-------|---------|-----------|-------|
| **First name** | "Meena" | Yes | Last name is never shown. First name only. Neighborly, not formal. |
| **Avatar** | Photo or generated avatar | Yes | Real photo encouraged but a friendly generated avatar is the default. Never a blank silhouette. |
| **Building/Tower** | "Tower B" | Yes | Not flat number. Enough to know they are close, not enough to find their door uninvited. |
| **Community rating** | 4.8 (from 23 neighbors) | After 3+ orders | Not shown until 3 orders completed. Before that, show "New cook in your community" badge. |
| **Member since** | "Sharing since Jan 2026" | Yes | Framed as "sharing since," not "registered since." |
| **Cooking style** (self-described) | "South Indian home cooking, weekend baking" | Optional | Free text, max 100 characters. The cook describes themselves. |
| **Popular dishes** | "Biryani, Mango Pickle, Banana Bread" | Auto-generated | Pulled from their most-requested listings. Not manually entered. |
| **Response time** | "Usually responds in 15 min" | Auto-generated | Calculated from chat response history. Helps set expectations. |

### 3.3 What Buyers Do NOT See

| Field | Why It Is Hidden |
|-------|-----------------|
| Full name | Privacy. First name is sufficient for the neighbor dynamic. |
| Flat/apartment number | Safety. Building is enough for proximity context. |
| Phone number | All communication goes through in-app chat until both parties choose to share contact info. |
| Kitchen photos | Creates false quality assurance. See reasoning above. |
| Certifications | We do not verify them, so displaying them would be misleading. |
| Exact earnings | Private financial information. |

### 3.4 What Admins Collect (Internal - Never Shown to Buyers)

| Field | Purpose | Collected When |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| Full legal name | Accountability in case of disputes or legal issues | Registration |
| Flat/apartment number | Verification of community membership, precise location for admin use | Registration |
| Phone number | Admin contact for urgent issues, account recovery | Registration |
| Government ID (optional) | For communities that require it, stored encrypted | Registration (if community policy requires it) |
| Community membership proof | Gate pass, society app screenshot, or admin vouching | Registration |
| Report history | Track complaints and patterns | Ongoing |
| Listing history | Audit trail | Ongoing |
| Chat flags | Automated flagging of concerning messages | Ongoing |

### 3.5 The "New Cook" Experience

When someone lists food for the first time, they do not have ratings yet. Here is how to handle the cold start:

- Show a **"New cook in your community"** badge in Coriander Green. Frame newness as exciting, not risky.
- Display: **"[Name] just started sharing food in [Community]. Be one of the first to try!"**
- After their first 3 completed orders with no reports, transition to the standard rating display.
- If a new cook receives a report before reaching 3 orders, flag for admin review.

## 4. Visual Language

### 4.1 Icon Style

**Style: Outlined with selective fills. Rounded corners. 1.5px stroke weight.**

The icon language should feel hand-drawn-adjacent but precise. Think: the care of a home cook, the clarity of a well-organized kitchen.

**Specifications:**
- Stroke weight: 1.5px (consistent across all icons).
- Corner radius: 2px minimum on all corners (no sharp points).
- Grid: 24x24px design grid with 2px padding.
- Color: Neutral 700 (Slate) by default, Turmeric for active/selected states.
- Style: Outlined for navigation and system icons. Filled with Turmeric for active states only.

**Icon Categories and Concepts:**

| Category | Icons Needed | Style Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Navigation | Home, Search, My Orders, Chat, Profile | Outlined default, Turmeric fill when active |
| Food | Plate, Bowl, Tiffin, Spoon, Flame | Slightly more illustrative, can break the strict grid slightly for warmth |
| Actions | Heart (save), Share, Report, Filter | Standard outlined |
| Status | Clock (time), Check (confirmed), X (cancelled) | Use semantic colors |
| Community | People, Building, Star (rating), Chat bubble | Rounded, friendly proportions |

**What to Avoid:**
- No sharp, geometric, tech-startup-style icons.
- No emoji-style icons. These are UI elements, not decorations.
- No inconsistent stroke weights. Every icon must use 1.5px.
- No filled icons in default states (except brand logo).

### 4.2 Image Treatment for Food Photos

Food photos are the single most important visual element in GharKa. They must look appetizing, honest, and homemade -- not styled like a restaurant menu.

**Photo Guidelines for Cooks:**
- Encourage natural lighting. Provide a simple tip on the upload screen: "Natural light makes your food look best. Try photographing near a window."
- No filters applied by the app. The food should look like it actually looks.
- Minimum resolution: 600x600px. The app should gently prompt if a photo is below this.
- Aspect ratio: Allow flexible aspect ratios but display in 4:3 crops on listing cards.

**App-Side Image Treatment:**
- Cards: 4:3 crop, 8px border-radius on top corners (matching card radius).
- Detail view: Full-width image, 16:9 or original aspect ratio, gentle parallax scroll.
- Subtle warm overlay: Apply a barely perceptible warm tint (+2% warmth in post-processing) to normalize photos taken under different lighting conditions. This should be invisible to the conscious eye but makes the overall feed feel cohesive.
- No aggressive saturation boosts. No "food photography" filters. Authenticity over aesthetics.
- Placeholder for no-photo listings: A warm illustrated plate with steam rising and the text "No photo yet -- ask [Name] about this dish."

### 4.3 Empty State Illustrations

Empty states are moments of delight, not dead ends. Each empty state gets a custom illustration in the brand style.

**Illustration Style:**
- Flat with subtle texture (paper-grain overlay at 5% opacity).
- Limited palette: Turmeric, Coriander, Neutral 200, and one accent per illustration.
- Characters have simple, friendly proportions (slightly oversized heads, minimal facial features -- a dot for eyes, a curve for a smile).
- Scene-based, not icon-based. Show a situation, not a symbol.

**Empty State Concepts:**

| Screen | Illustration Concept | Copy |
|--------|---------------------|------|
| Home feed (no listings) | Two neighbors talking over a balcony railing, one holding a pot | "No one's cooking right now. Check back soon -- or be the first to share!" |
| Search (no results) | A person peeking into an empty tiffin carrier with a curious expression | "No dishes match your search. Try something else, or browse what's fresh today." |
| My Orders (no orders yet) | A plate and spoon waiting on a doorstep mat that says "Welcome" | "No orders yet. Your neighbor's kitchen is waiting." |
| Chat (no conversations) | Two empty speech bubbles with a small heart between them | "No chats yet. Start a conversation with a cook whose food catches your eye." |
| Saved (no saved items) | An empty fridge with a sticky note that says "add favorites here" | "Nothing saved yet. Tap the heart on any dish to save it for later." |
| Cook's listing page (new cook, no items) | A person in a kitchen with arms spread wide, empty counter in front of them | "Your kitchen is ready. Share your first dish with the community." |

### 4.4 Loading State Animations

**Philosophy: Small, warm, and never longer than necessary.**

- **Primary loader**: A small pot (outlined, Turmeric color) with three steam wisps that gently animate in a wave pattern. Loop time: 1.5 seconds. Used for full-screen loading states.
- **Inline loader**: Three dots in Turmeric that pulse in sequence (not a spinner). Used for loading more content, sending messages, etc.
- **Skeleton screens**: For listing feeds and profiles, use skeleton loading (gray placeholder shapes that pulse gently). Skeleton shapes should match the actual content layout precisely. Pulse color: Neutral 200 to Neutral 100, 1-second cycle.
- **Pull-to-refresh**: The steaming pot animation plays during pull-to-refresh, with a subtle "Checking what's cooking..." text.

**What to Avoid:**
- No spinners. Spinners feel technical and cold.
- No progress bars (unless uploading a photo, where progress is genuinely useful).
- No loading animations longer than 3 seconds without a text update explaining the delay.

### 4.5 Micro-Interaction Style

**Philosophy: Smooth with subtle springiness. Never robotic, never bouncy to the point of distraction.**

**Animation Curve**: Custom ease -- `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)` for elements that "arrive" (modals, toasts, new cards). Standard `ease-out` for elements that "leave."

**Specific Interactions:**

| Interaction | Animation | Duration | Notes |
|-------------|-----------|----------|-------|
| Tap a listing card | Card scales to 0.97 on press, 1.0 on release with spring ease | 150ms | Feels tactile, like pressing a real thing |
| Heart/save a dish | Heart fills from center outward in Terracotta, with a tiny burst of 3 small circles | 300ms | The only "delightful" animation. Keep it small. |
| Send order request | Button morphs into a checkmark with a gentle bounce | 400ms | Confirms the action without a full-screen change |
| New message received | Chat icon gets a subtle pulse (scale 1.0 to 1.1 and back) with badge count animating in | 200ms | Noticeable but not distracting |
| Pull to refresh | Pot animation with increasing steam as you pull further down | Variable | Steam intensity maps to pull distance |
| Toast notifications | Slide in from top with spring ease, auto-dismiss after 3s with fade-out | 300ms in, 200ms out | Never blocks content below |
| Page transitions | Shared element transitions where possible (food photo from card to detail view) | 250ms | Makes navigation feel spatial and connected |

## 5. Voice and Tone Guide

### 5.1 How GharKa Speaks

GharKa speaks like a thoughtful neighbor -- someone who is helpful without being overbearing, warm without being fake, and clear without being blunt. The app is not a brand shouting at users. It is a quiet, well-designed tool that gets out of the way while maintaining a human touch.

**Voice Principles (constant across all contexts):**

| Principle | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **First-name basis** | We use the user's first name. We refer to cooks by first name. No "Dear User" or "valued customer." | "Meena confirmed your order" not "The seller has confirmed your order" |
| **Active, not passive** | Things happen. People do things. We do not hide behind passive constructions. | "We couldn't find that" not "No results were found" |
| **Short sentences** | Mobile screens are small. Attention is limited. Say it in fewer words. | "Ready in 20 min." not "This dish will be prepared and ready for pickup in approximately 20 minutes." |
| **Hindi-English blend** | Where natural, use common Hindi words that even non-Hindi speakers in Indian gated communities understand. | "Ghar ka khana" in taglines. "Pakka confirmed" in casual contexts. Never forced. |
| **Explain, don't instruct** | Tell people why, not just what. | "Add a photo -- dishes with photos get 3x more requests" not "Photo required" |

### 5.2 Tone Variations

The voice stays the same. The tone shifts based on context.

| Context | Tone | Temperature | Example |
|---------|------|-------------|---------|
| Onboarding | Welcoming, excited | Warm | "Welcome to the neighborhood kitchen." |
| Browsing | Appetizing, casual | Warm-neutral | "Fresh today from your neighbors." |
| Ordering | Clear, reassuring | Neutral | "Your request has been sent to Meena." |
| Waiting | Patient, informative | Neutral | "Meena usually responds within 15 minutes." |
| Success | Celebratory but brief | Warm | "All set! Enjoy your meal." |
| Errors | Honest, helpful | Neutral-cool | "Something went wrong. Let's try that again." |
| Disclaimers | Honest, friendly | Neutral | "We connect neighbors. The food is between you two." |
| Empty states | Encouraging, light | Warm | "Nothing here yet. Check back soon -- or start cooking!" |

### 5.3 Error Messages

Error messages are where most apps lose their humanity. GharKa never blames the user and always offers a next step.

| Error Scenario | Bad (Generic) | Good (GharKa) |
|----------------|---------------|----------------|
| Network failure | "Network error. Please try again." | "Looks like you're offline. We'll try again when you're connected." |
| Failed to send order request | "Order failed. Error code 500." | "We couldn't send your request to [Name]. Check your connection and try again." |
| Photo upload failed | "Upload failed." | "That photo didn't go through. Maybe try a smaller file or check your Wi-Fi?" |
| Login failed | "Invalid credentials." | "That didn't match. Double-check your number and try again." |
| Cook unavailable | "Seller is currently unavailable." | "[Name] isn't taking orders right now. They might be back soon -- save this dish for later?" |
| Listing expired | "This listing is no longer available." | "This dish is no longer available. Check out what else is fresh today." |
| Location not supported | "Your area is not supported." | "GharKa isn't in your community yet. Want us to let you know when it arrives?" |

### 5.4 Success Messages

Success moments are brief celebrations. Never over the top.

| Success Scenario | Copy |
|------------------|------|
| Order request sent | "Sent! [Name] will get back to you shortly." |
| Order confirmed by cook | "[Name] confirmed your order. Pickup at [time]." |
| First listing posted (cook) | "Your first dish is live! Your neighbors can see it now." |
| Profile setup complete | "You're all set. Time to see what's cooking." |
| Review submitted | "Thanks for the feedback. It helps the whole community." |
| Saved a dish | "Saved. You'll find it in your favorites." |
| Joined a community | "Welcome to [Community Name]. Here's what your neighbors are cooking." |

### 5.5 Empty State Copy

Already detailed in Section 4.3. Key principles:
- Always suggest a next action.
- Never just say "nothing here."
- Use the illustration to carry the emotional weight; keep the copy short.

### 5.6 Notification Copy Style

Notifications are the most intimate touchpoint. They appear on the user's lock screen, between personal messages and calendar reminders. They must earn that space.

**Principles:**
- Lead with the person, not the action. "[Name]" first, then what happened.
- Keep it under 60 characters for the title line.
- Never use ALL CAPS or exclamation marks in notifications.
- Include enough context to be useful without opening the app.

| Notification Type | Title | Body |
|-------------------|-------|------|
| Order confirmed | "[Name] confirmed your order" | "Pickup at [time] from [Building]. [Dish name]." |
| New message | "[Name] sent you a message" | "[Preview of first 40 characters...]" |
| Listing from saved cook | "[Name] is cooking today" | "[Dish name] -- available for pickup at [time]." |
| Order request received (cook) | "New request from [Name]" | "[Dish name] -- tap to confirm or decline." |
| Reminder to pick up | "Pickup reminder" | "Your [dish name] from [Name] is ready at [time]." |
| New cook in community | "New cook in [Community]" | "[Name] just shared their first dish. Check it out." |
| Weekly digest | "This week in [Community]" | "[X] dishes shared, [Y] new cooks. See what's fresh." |

## 6. Community Building Through UI

### 6.1 Making Users Feel Like Neighbors, Not Customers

The single biggest design risk for GharKa is accidentally building a food delivery app. Every decision must be stress-tested against this question: "Does this make people feel like neighbors or like customers?"

**Design Decisions That Reinforce Neighbor Dynamic:**

| Element | Marketplace Approach (Avoid) | Neighbor Approach (GharKa) |
|---------|------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Seller label | "Seller" or "Vendor" | "Cook" or just their first name |
| Browse page title | "Menu" or "Restaurants" | "What's cooking today" or "Fresh from your neighbors" |
| Order action | "Add to cart" / "Order now" | "Request this dish" |
| Payment | Integrated checkout | "Settle with [Name] directly" |
| Rating prompt | "Rate your order" | "How was [Name]'s [dish name]?" |
| Seller page | "Store" | "[Name]'s Kitchen" |
| Communication | "Contact seller" | "Message [Name]" |
| Cancel action | "Cancel order" | "Let [Name] know you can't make it" |

### 6.2 Social Proof Elements

Social proof in GharKa is not about conversion metrics. It is about community participation -- showing that real neighbors are cooking and eating together.

**Community Activity Feed (Home Screen, Top Section)**

A horizontal scrollable strip showing recent community activity:
- "[Name] shared Butter Chicken -- 4 neighbors requested it"
- "[Name] just joined as a cook"
- "12 dishes shared this week in [Community Name]"

**Design**: Small avatars, one-line text, Neutral 700, subtle dividers. Scrolls horizontally. Not tappable (to avoid turning it into a leaderboard).

**Listing-Level Social Proof**

On each food listing card:
- "[X] neighbors enjoyed this" (shown after 5+ completed orders for that dish).
- "[Name] and [Name] from your building tried this" (if friends/building-mates have ordered).
- "Popular this week" badge for dishes with 10+ requests in 7 days.

**Cook-Level Social Proof**

On cook profiles:
- "Sharing food with [X] neighbors since [month/year]"
- "Known for: [top 3 dishes by order count]"
- Individual reviews show reviewer's first name and building (never anonymous, since this is a community -- accountability matters).

### 6.3 Community Warmth Indicators

Small design details that make the app feel alive and lived-in.

**"Cooking Now" Indicator**

When a cook is actively preparing food (they've set a listing as "being prepared"), show a small animated steam icon next to their name in the feed. This creates a sense of real-time life -- someone nearby is in their kitchen right now, making food.

**Community Stats (Settings or About Page)**

A simple, beautifully designed section:
- "In [Community Name], [X] neighbors have shared [Y] homemade dishes."
- "That's [Z] meals that didn't come from a delivery app."
- Updated weekly. Framed as a community achievement, not a platform metric.

**Seasonal and Festival Touches**

- During major festivals (Diwali, Eid, Christmas, Pongal), subtly adjust the color of the header area to include a festive element -- a single diya icon, a crescent, a star. Nothing garish. A single, small, tasteful element.
- Prompt cooks with: "Making something special for [festival]? Your neighbors would love to know."
- These should be inclusive and cover the major festivals relevant to the community's demographics. Default to the most common festivals in Indian gated communities. Allow community admins to customize.

### 6.4 "Made With Love" -- Without Being Cheesy

The temptation to plaster hearts and "made with love" everywhere is strong. Resist it. Instead, let the warmth come through in structural choices, not decorative additions.

**What to do:**

- **Use the cook's name everywhere.** "Meena's Rajma" is inherently warmer than "Rajma (Home Style)." The name IS the warmth.
- **Show the "why they cook" line.** During cook onboarding, ask: "In one line, why do you love cooking?" Display this on their profile in Caveat (handwritten font), in quotes. Examples:
  - *"My mother's recipes shouldn't stay in just one kitchen."*
  - *"I bake because it makes people smile."*
  - *"Everyone asks for my pickle. Now they don't have to ask."*
- **"First dish" celebration.** When a cook shares their first ever listing, their card gets a subtle Coriander Green border and a small "First time sharing" badge. This celebrates the act of sharing, not the food itself.
- **Thank you loop.** After a buyer picks up food, prompt them (once, gently): "Want to say thanks to [Name]?" with a simple message input. Not a review. Not a rating. Just a thank-you message that goes to the cook's chat. This is the single most powerful "made with love" feature -- closing the loop with gratitude.

**What NOT to do:**
- No heart icons on every card. The heart is reserved for the "save/favorite" action only.
- No "made with love" text generated by the app. If a cook wants to write "made with love" in their description, that is their choice. The app never says it for them.
- No fake "handmade" or "artisan" labels. This is not a craft marketplace. It is neighbors cooking.
- No animated hearts, sparkles, or confetti. The warmth is structural, not decorative.

## 7. Brand Protection and Compliance

### 7.1 Trademark Strategy

- Register "GharKa" as a wordmark in Class 42 (technology services) and Class 43 (food services/temporary accommodation) in India.
- Register the logo (once finalized) as a device mark.
- Secure the domain name and social handles: @gharka on Instagram, Twitter/X, and Facebook. gharka.in and gharka.app domains.
- File a trademark watch to monitor similar names in the food-tech space.

### 7.2 Brand Usage Rules

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Name | Always "GharKa" -- capital G, capital K, no space. Never "Gharka," "ghar ka," "Ghar Ka," or "GHARKA." |
| Logo | Minimum clear space equal to the height of the "G" on all sides. Never stretch, rotate, or recolor. |
| Colors | Primary Turmeric must not be modified. No gradients on brand colors unless specified in these guidelines. |
| Fonts | Nunito and Inter only in all official materials. No substitutions without brand team approval. |
| Photography | No stock photos of food in any official communication. All food photos must be of actual homemade food. |

### 7.3 Legal Positioning

GharKa must consistently position itself as a **facilitator**, not a marketplace. This is not just a branding choice -- it is a legal protection strategy.

**In all public-facing copy:**
- Use "connect" and "facilitate," never "sell" or "deliver."
- Use "cook" and "neighbor," never "vendor" or "seller."
- Use "request," never "order" in contexts where it implies guaranteed fulfillment.
- Use "community," never "marketplace" or "platform" (though "app" is fine).

**In terms of service:**
- Explicitly state that GharKa is an intermediary facilitator.
- Explicitly state that no employment, agency, or partnership relationship exists between GharKa and any cook.
- Explicitly state that GharKa does not process, hold, or transfer funds.
- Explicitly disclaim liability for food quality, safety, allergens, and transaction disputes.

### 7.4 Brand Monitoring

- Conduct quarterly reviews of all user-facing screens against this brand guide.
- Monitor app store reviews and social mentions for brand perception issues.
- Track disclaimer acknowledgment rates during onboarding (target: 100% of users see and acknowledge Level 1 disclaimer before accessing listings).
- Survey users semi-annually on brand perception: "What is GharKa?" Correct answer: "An app that connects me with home cooks in my community." Red flag answer: "A food delivery app."
